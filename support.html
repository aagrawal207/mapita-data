Support

Questions, problems, or a fact that is wrong? Write to aagrawal207@gmail.com. You will get an answer from the person who makes the app.

Report a mistake

A place's details and the foot of Getting around and Good to know have a Something wrong? Tell us link. It opens an email with the guide and the place already filled in. Say what is wrong or missing, and send it. Nothing is sent until you press Send.

Ask for a new place or a fresh check

The foot of the list of places has Ask for a place, and a search that finds nothing offers to ask for what you typed. Getting around and Good to know end with Going soon? Ask for a fresh check, for a guide you would like checked again before your trip. Both open an email with the details filled in. Each new place is researched and checked by hand, so it takes a while, but every request is read.

How Mapita works

  • Choose a place from the list when the app opens. Tap its name at the top of the map to change it.
  • The map shows the towns and areas, the places, and the drive times between them. Pinch to zoom, and tap a pin or an area's name for a card. It opens on the areas, with a list of them under the map: choose one to see its places. The filters at the top show one kind of place at a time.
  • Search finds everything the guide says about a word or about one area.
  • Directions open in Apple Maps or Google Maps, as you choose.
  • Links in the guide, such as a website to pay a fee, open with a tap. Long-press a tip or a fact to copy it.
  • Ask an AI app (on a place, an area, a trip or a Good to know topic) shares the guide's words with a short question to an app such as ChatGPT, so you can ask more.
  • Without a connection the maps and guides still work. Photos you have seen once stay on the phone.

Keeping the guides current

Each guide says the month it was checked. Prices, closures and rules are checked again on a schedule, and the app fetches newer guides by itself when it has a connection. A note that holds only until a certain day, such as a closure, disappears from the app after that day. Rules and prices change, so check what matters to you before you go.

Where the maps and photos come from

  • Maps are drawn from OpenStreetMap data, © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the Open Database License. The simplified map data is published in this repository.
  • Photos come from Wikimedia Commons under free licences. Each photo shows its author and licence, and a tap opens its page.
  • The US travel advisory level comes from the US State Department.
